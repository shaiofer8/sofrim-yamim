## Findings

- **Google's live Play Console Help page confirms verbatim: "Developers with personal accounts created after November 13, 2023, must run a closed test for their app with a minimum of 12 testers who have been opted in continuously for at least 14 days" before production access.** [Source: Google, "App testing requirements for new personal developer accounts", Play Console Help (live page, directly fetched), accessed 2026-08-17, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en]. Confidence: high (primary source, directly fetched and quoted this session). Class: policy.
- **Google's live help page also confirms: "Testers who opt in, test for fewer than 14 days, and then opt out do not count toward the requirement. If a tester opts out and opts back in later, the 14 days must be consecutive to count toward the minimum requirement of 12 continuous opted-in testers."** [Source: Google, "App testing requirements for new personal developer accounts", Play Console Help, accessed 2026-08-17, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en]. Confidence: high (primary source). Class: policy.
- **Google's live "Get started with Play Console" help page confirms verbatim: "There is a US$25 one-time registration fee that you can pay with the following credit or debit cards" (MasterCard, Visa, American Express, Discover, and Visa Electron outside the U.S.).** [Source: Google, "Get started with Play Console", Play Console Help (live page, directly fetched), accessed 2026-08-17, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6112435?hl=en]. Confidence: high (primary source, directly fetched and quoted this session). Class: policy.
- **The same official Google page states identity verification may require "a valid government ID and a credit card, both under your legal name," and separately confirms that starting in early 2024, new personal-account developers must verify access to an Android device using the Play Console mobile app before making an app public.** [Source: Google, "Get started with Play Console", Play Console Help, accessed 2026-08-17, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6112435?hl=en]. Confidence: high (primary source, directly fetched). Class: policy. — This corroborates the round-1 secondary-source claim (testerbee.com) about device verification, satisfying the two-source rule with one primary + one secondary source.
- **A default/common "remove ads" one-time IAP price cited for utility-style apps is $1.99, with real-world ad-blocker/utility examples clustering at $1.99–$2.99 (e.g., "Purify" ad blocker at $1.99, comparable utility apps at $2.99).** [Source: AppsGeyser Help Center, "Setting Up the 'Remove Ads' Product in Your App" (app-builder platform documentation, describes its own default price) + App Store listing data for "Purify: Block Ads and Tracking", accessed 2026-08-17, https://support.appsgeyser.com/hc/en-us/articles/6973084966172-Setting-Up-the-Remove-Ads-Product-in-Your-App and https://apps.apple.com/us/app/purify-block-ads-and-tracking/id1030156203]. Confidence: medium (two independent sources converge on the ~$1.99–$2.99 band, but one is a platform's own default-price documentation rather than market data, and neither is a broad benchmark study). Class: pricing.
- **RevenueCat's "State of Subscription Apps 2025" reports that 35% of apps now mix subscriptions with consumable or lifetime one-time purchases (a hybrid model), led by Gaming (61.7% of apps) and Social & Lifestyle (39.4%); Health & Fitness and Productivity apps remain mostly subscription-only with consumables under-utilized.** [Source: RevenueCat, "State of Subscription Apps 2025", 2025, accessed 2026-08-17, https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025]. Confidence: medium (primary industry-data source with large sample — 115,000+ apps per secondary summary — but figures relayed via search snippet, not independently re-verified by direct fetch of the report itself; also note this dataset is subscription-app-centric, so may not generalize well to a free-with-banner-ads utility app). Class: pricing.
- **A widely-cited (but not freshly-dated) figure states roughly 15.7% of a gaming/app audience says it would pay to remove ads, rising to 24% among "zoomers," versus 30.4% preferring traditional IAP and 31.4% willing to watch rewarded ads.** [Source: aggregated in Medium/Udonis-style mobile-marketing roundups citing unnamed survey data, accessed 2026-08-17 — no single confirmable primary source found]. Confidence: low (unsourced numbers relayed through a secondary aggregator; single-source, no named original study identified; treat as unverified single-source). Class: pricing.
- **AdMob/mobile ad benchmark aggregators report the Middle East region as a low-tier eCPM market: banner eCPM around $0.10 (vs. Tier-1 banner eCPMs of $0.50–$1.50), and rewarded-video eCPM around $1.19 for the 2021–2022 period; average revenue-per-user in the Middle East is characterized as "almost non-existent" at $0.001–$0.002.** [Source: AdPumb, "Mobile eCPM Report Across the World 2023 – Q1 2023", 2023, accessed 2026-08-17, https://adpumb.com/blog/mobile-ecpm-report-across-the-world/]. Confidence: low (single source, data is 2021–2023 vintage — exceeds the 3-month policy/12-month benchmark freshness bar by a wide margin — and "Middle East" as a region is not Israel-specific; Israel likely differs materially from lower-income MENA states in eCPM given its high smartphone penetration and ad spend). Class: pricing.
- **A November 2024 Israel-specific publisher CPM report (not AdMob-branded, covers AdSense/Facebook Audience Network/programmatic) gives Israeli display-ad CPM ranges of roughly $1.43–$7.14 (mobile display), $2.29–$11.43 (desktop display), and $1.71–$8.57 specifically for Google AdSense display — meaningfully higher than the generic "Middle East" $0.10 banner-eCPM figure from the 2023 AdPumb report, though these are CPM (not eCPM) ranges and AdMob is not named in the source.** [Source: PublisherGrowth, "Ad CPM Rates in Israel for Publishers", 2024-11-21, accessed 2026-08-17, https://publishergrowth.com/blog-details/ad-cpm-rates-in-israel-for-publishers]. Confidence: low-medium (single source for Israel specifically, ~21 months old relative to current date so it fails the 12-month benchmark freshness bar; also a different ad-format/network mix than AdMob banner/interstitial — flagged as a contradiction with the generic MENA figure below). Class: pricing.
- **Hebrew-language ASO guidance (ApptWeak, published/updated 2024-02-27) recommends localizing app-store metadata into Hebrew rather than relying on English keywords, noting Hebrew-keyword search results differ substantially from English-keyword results for the same query, Hebrew keyword-difficulty scores tend to be lower (a ranking opportunity), and that as of the article's writing only 4 of the top 30 Israeli apps had fully localized App Store product pages — implying low competition for early localizers.** [Source: ApptWeak, "How to localize your app in Hebrew & Arabic?", updated 2024-02-27, accessed 2026-08-17, https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/why-you-should-localize-your-aso-in-hebrew-arabic]. Confidence: medium (single named ASO-industry source, directly fetched; article is now ~2.5 years old, exceeding the 12-month freshness bar for a GTM benchmark, so the "4 of 30" competitive-gap figure should be treated as dated/directional rather than current). Class: gtm.
- **Israel is characterized (in a secondary aggregator citation, not independently verified) as a market with especially high app engagement — "adults using apps for an average of 80 minutes per day" — and Google AdSense is described as popular among Israeli publishers partly due to competitive rates on Hebrew-language content for native-speaker audiences.** [Source: secondary aggregator summary citing unnamed mobile-ad-report data, accessed 2026-08-17, referenced via https://maf.ad/en/blog/mobile-ads-ecpm/ and related search results]. Confidence: low (unsourced figure, single mention, could not trace to an original named study in this round). Class: gtm.
- **Israel-focused Facebook groups (e.g., city-based "Secret Tel Aviv"/"Secret Jerusalem"-style groups, with reportedly 500+ English-language Israel-focused groups) exist as a community channel, and a separate, largely non-overlapping ecosystem of Hebrew-language Facebook groups exists in parallel — but no case study or data was found on using these groups to launch/market a niche Hebrew utility app specifically.** [Source: Daniel Rosehill via Medium, "Israel Focused Facebook Groups For English Speakers", undated, accessed 2026-08-17, https://medium.com/living-in-israel/israel-focused-facebook-groups-for-english-speakers-ddb2732ebbba]. Confidence: low (single source, anecdotal/listicle, no marketing-outcome data, and covers English-speaker groups more than Hebrew-native groups relevant to this app). Class: gtm.
- **Product Hunt is described by 2026 launch-guide content as strong for SaaS/tools/dev-tools audiences but explicitly noted to "struggle with solutions to niche problems without pre-existing mindshare," with successful launches typically built on 6–8 weeks of pre-launch audience-building rather than launch-day virality alone.** [Source: Blazon Agency, "How to Launch on Product Hunt: The Complete Guide for 2026", 2026, accessed 2026-08-17, https://blazonagency.com/post/how-to-launch-on-product-hunt]. Confidence: low (single marketing-agency source, no outcome data/case studies with real numbers for niche utility apps). Class: gtm.

## Leads

- Contradiction to flag for the synthesis: the generic "Middle East" AdMob-style eCPM figure (~$0.10 banner eCPM, from a 2023 aggregator) is roughly an order of magnitude lower than the Israel-specific display CPM range (~$1.43–$7.14 mobile) from the November 2024 PublisherGrowth report. These are not apples-to-apples (different ad formats/networks/metrics, and eCPM ≠ CPM), but the gap is large enough that the app's actual AdMob banner eCPM in Israel is genuinely uncertain — recommend the product/business-model dimension treat AdMob revenue-per-user as a wide-range unknown (plausibly $0.10–$2 banner eCPM) rather than pin a single number, and note this needs a dedicated AdMob-specific (not general ad-network) Israel data point that this round could not find.
- No source in either round gave AdMob-branded (as opposed to AdSense/generic-network) eCPM data for Israel specifically — worth another round with queries like "AdMob mediation Israel eCPM report" or checking AdMob's own published country-tier lists if Google publishes one.
- The RevenueCat "State of Subscription Apps" report is subscription-app-centric (its 115,000+ app sample skews toward apps that already monetize via subscription); its 35% hybrid-monetization figure may not transfer well to a free/ad-supported utility app and should be flagged as a soft analogy, not a direct comparable, in any pricing recommendation.
- The "15.7% would pay to remove ads" statistic could not be traced to a named original study in this round — recommend not using it as a decision input; it should be re-flagged as unverified single-source if it resurfaces.
- Google Play production-access review timeline ("usually 7 days or less") was relayed via secondary search summary in round 1 and not independently re-confirmed by direct quote from the primary page's exact wording in round 2 — worth a follow-up direct fetch/search of that specific clause if precise SLA language matters for planning.
- No influencer/creator-partnership case studies or data were found for Israeli Hebrew-market app marketing in either round — this remains an open gap; likely needs a dimension-specific or Hebrew-language search (not run here, as this brief's queries were run in English) to surface local Israeli tech-influencer or app-review-channel data.
