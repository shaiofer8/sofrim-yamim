# Customer Segments & Behavior — Round 3

## Findings

- **Israel's Jewish population splits roughly into four religiosity segments, consistently reported across multiple years of CBS (Israel Central Bureau of Statistics) data**: approximately 42–45% secular (חילוני), 25–33% traditional (מסורתי), 11–16% religious (דתי), and 11–14% ultra-Orthodox/Haredi (חרדי). A 2025 (Rosh Hashanah) figure reported 42.7% secular / 33.5% traditional / 12.0% religious / 11.4% ultra-Orthodox; a 2021 CBS-sourced figure (published in Hebrew coverage) reported 45.3% non-religious/secular vs. 54.3% traditional+religious+ultra-Orthodox combined (33.1% / 10.7% / 10.5%); a 2016 CBS figure reported ~45% secular / ~25% traditional / ~16% religious / ~14% ultra-Orthodox. [Sources: (1) The Jerusalem Post, "Israel's annual stats: 2025 population, economy, society", 2025, accessed 2026-08-17, https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/article-867765; (2) Bizportal, "הלמ\"ס: מרבית אוכלוסיית ישראל אינה חילונית" (citing CBS 2021 survey), accessed 2026-08-17, https://www.bizportal.co.il/general/news/article/745287]. Confidence: medium-high — two independent Israeli publishers reporting figures that both trace to CBS and are directionally consistent across three separate years (2016/2021/2025), satisfying the two-source rule for the general shape of the breakdown (roughly 55%+ of Israeli Jews identify as traditional/religious/Haredi to some degree, not purely secular), though exact percentages vary slightly by survey year/methodology. Class: segment. **This is a key segment-sizing input**: the majority-non-secular share of the Jewish population (traditional + religious + Haredi, ~55-58%) represents the core plausible demand base for a Jewish/Israeli-holiday-focused countdown app, beyond purely secular users who may only care about national/civil holidays (Independence Day, Memorial Day) rather than religious observance dates.

- **No second independent analytics provider (Sensor Tower, data.ai) was found this session publishing Israel-specific calendar-app category rankings** to cross-verify the Similarweb Round-2 findings — Sensor Tower's Israel-specific public blog posts found cover Communication and Lifestyle categories (2024), not Calendar/Productivity. [Source: Sensor Tower blog index, accessed 2026-08-17, https://sensortower.com/blog/2024-q3-unified-top-5-communication%20apps-revenue-il-6070aae1241bc16eb81f5bab and https://sensortower.com/blog/2024-q1-unified-top-5-lifestyle-units-il-600b3087241bc16eb8090944]. Class: other (evidence gap, not a claim).

## Leads

- The ~55-58% "traditional or more religious" share of Israeli Jews is a meaningfully larger addressable base than a naive assumption of "only the religious/Haredi minority cares about Jewish holidays" — worth carrying into positioning/pricing discussions (a holiday-countdown app has a plausible majority-of-Jewish-population audience for at least some Jewish holidays, not just a small religious niche), though secular Israelis' engagement is likely concentrated on national/civil holidays and major Jewish holidays (Rosh Hashanah, Pesach, Yom Kippur) rather than the full religious calendar (Shabbat times, minor fasts, etc.).
- Arab-Israeli population religiosity (from round-2016 CBS data glimpsed in search snippets: ~11% secular, ~57% traditional, ~31% religious among Arabs) surfaced incidentally — not pursued further since this app's Hebrew-RTL Jewish/Israeli-holiday focus makes this population likely out of scope, but worth a note for the positioning dimension if a broader "Israeli holidays" (vs. strictly "Jewish holidays") framing is considered.

- Any data connecting religiosity segment directly to app-buying behavior or willingness to pay for a "remove ads" IAP (no source found linking religiosity/demographic segment to in-app-purchase conversion behavior specifically for calendar/countdown apps).
