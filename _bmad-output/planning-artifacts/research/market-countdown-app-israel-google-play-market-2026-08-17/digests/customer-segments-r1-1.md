# Customer Segments & Behavior — Round 1

## Findings

- **The leading Hebrew-calendar app ("הלוח העברי" / "Hebrew Calendar") self-reports 3,000,000+ downloads and "hundreds of thousands" of monthly active users**, positioning itself as a comprehensive Jewish-content app (calendar, Shabbat/prayer times, daily Torah study, holidays, WhatsApp stickers). [Source: Hebcal.co.il (developer's own site), homepage marketing copy, accessed 2026-08-17, https://hebcal.co.il/?lang=en]. Confidence: low (single-source, self-reported by the app's own developer — no independent corroboration of the download/MAU figures found this session). Class: segment.

- **The same app's Israel App Store listing shows 4.5★ from 14,000 ratings**, categorized under "שירותים בסיסיים" (Basic/Utilities). [Source: Apple App Store (IL), app listing id356559317, accessed 2026-08-17, https://apps.apple.com/il/app/הלוח-העברי-לוח-שנה-יהודי/id356559317]. Confidence: high for the rating/count itself (primary storefront data), but **contradicts** the developer's own marketing page which separately cites "30,000 ratings" and 4.7★ — likely an aggregate across Android+iOS+other locales rather than the IL App Store alone. Class: segment.

- **WhatsApp added a native group "events" feature (event creation, RSVP, automatic reminders as the event approaches)**, announced for rollout starting with Communities and then "expected to reach all groups." [Source: TGSpot, "חדש בוואטסאפ: קבוצות לארגון אירועים ואישור השתתפות ישירות מתוך האפליקציה", 2024-05-03, accessed 2026-08-17, https://www.tgspot.co.il/communities-events-and-replies-in-announcement-groups/]. Confidence: medium (single Israeli tech-press source reporting a confirmed product launch, but article does not confirm Israel-specific rollout timing, and >2 years old relative to "current" so should be re-verified for present-day ubiquity). Class: behavior — this is a plausible existing substitute habit (WhatsApp group event reminders) competing with a dedicated countdown app, though it does not include Hebrew/Jewish holiday data.

- **A 2018 ECAL survey of 1,000 Australian adults (ages 18–64)** found 70% rely most on a digital calendar to manage their life (46.7% mobile app, 23.3% desktop); males 73% vs females 67% reliance; age-group reliance ranged 62.8% (55–64) to 75.2% (35–44); 32.1% of females vs 24.6% of males still rely most on a paper diary. [Source: ECAL, "70% of adults rely on digital calendar", 2018 (exact date not stated on page), accessed 2026-08-17, https://ecal.com/70-percent-of-adults-rely-on-digital-calendar/]. Confidence: low — outside the 2-year freshness bar (historical context only), vendor-produced (ECAL is a calendar-marketing platform with commercial interest in these stats), and only one underlying source (aggregator sites like llcbuddy.com and explodingtopics.com merely recycle this same ECAL figure, so it does not satisfy the two-source rule). Class: behavior. **Unverified single-source; treat as historical context, not current fact.**

- **ECAL's 2025 "Buying Back Time" report** claims 82.7% of consumers using any scheduling solution are now "mobile-first" (up from a claimed 47% the prior year), 72% use a digital calendar as their primary planning tool, "Personal Health" is users' top calendar priority (75.4%, ahead of work scheduling), and Gen Z uses calendars for Social Events (62%) and Academics (37%). [Source: ECAL, "The 82.7% Mobile Mandate: Calendar vs. Digital Chaos" / 2025 Annual Report "Buying Back Time", 2025, accessed 2026-08-17, https://ecal.com/the-82-7-mobile-calendar-report-2025/]. Confidence: low — vendor marketing report, no disclosed sample size/methodology on the page, single-source. Class: behavior. **Unverified single-source.**

- **Countdown apps across app stores are marketed toward a consistent set of use cases**: weddings, birthdays, travel/vacations, pregnancy due-dates, exam/school deadlines, and holidays (Christmas/New Year prominently; no Jewish-holiday-specific dedicated app found among the English-language results). Drawn from consistent descriptions across many independent app listings and roundup articles (RankRed, Geekflare, Filmora, cellularnews, littletimer.org). Confidence: low (marketing copy / app-store descriptions, not user research) but the pattern is consistent across many independent listings, which supports it directionally as a segment map. Class: segment.

- **Claimed reasons users prefer a dedicated countdown app over a calendar/reminder app**: always-visible lock-screen/home-screen widgets, emotional/visual engagement (custom icons, colors, themes), reduced need to manually check a calendar, and stackable multiple reminders (e.g., 1 week before + 1 day before). [Source: multiple countdown-app marketing/blog pages, e.g. Geekflare "7 Best Countdown Apps for Events", littletimer.org blog, accessed 2026-08-17]. Confidence: low (vendor/marketing framing, not empirical research). Class: behavior.

- **A small, non-representative student project survey** (University of Southampton "gapapp" project) found predicted interest skewed to ages 18–25 (36.4% aged 18–20, 40.9% aged 21–23, 22.7% aged 24–25) for a countdown-type app aimed at university students. [Source: University of Southampton, "gapapp" project blog, "User Surveys", accessed 2026-08-17, https://generic.wordpress.soton.ac.uk/gapapp/?p=168]. Confidence: low (tiny sample size, single student project, app-specific, not generalizable to Israeli market or holiday-tracking use case). Class: segment.

- **Pregnancy/baby countdown apps show anecdotal habitual daily engagement** — app-store reviews and descriptions describe users checking the app "morning and evening" out of emotional anticipation, with common feature bundling (due-date countdown + fetal development info + to-do lists). [Source: various App Store / Google Play listings for pregnancy countdown apps (e.g. "Pregnancy Countdown: Baby Care", "Baby Countdown"), accessed 2026-08-17]. Confidence: low (user reviews/marketing copy, not a formal study). Class: behavior.

## Leads

- **Contradiction to chase**: הלוח העברי app's self-reported "30,000 ratings / 4.7★" (developer marketing page) vs. the actual Israel App Store listing showing "14,000 ratings / 4.5★". Possible explanation: the marketing figure aggregates Android + iOS + all country storefronts. Worth checking the Google Play listing for this app directly to reconcile (would matter for sizing the addressable Israeli Jewish-calendar-app audience).
- **WhatsApp group "Events" feature** (native RSVP + reminders, announced May 2024) is a plausible free substitute for parts of what a countdown/event app offers — but it lacks built-in Hebrew calendar/Jewish holiday data. This is more a competitive-dimension lead than a customer-segment one, but it also implies Israeli users already have a free, familiar tool for ad-hoc group event reminders, which narrows the differentiated need to holiday-specific + personal (non-group) countdown tracking.
- **AppsFlyer** (Israeli-founded mobile marketing analytics company) or other Israel-based mobile analytics firms may publish local app-category usage reports (calendar/productivity) — not checked this session, worth a targeted look in round 2.
- **Hebcal.com** (the original, English-oriented Jewish calendar website/API, distinct from hebcal.co.il above) was found in search results but not fetched for usage/traffic stats — worth checking directly (its GitHub/blog might disclose API call volume as a rough proxy for Jewish-calendar digital demand).
- No dedicated "countdown to Rosh Hashanah / Pesach" or Israeli-holiday-specific countdown app was surfaced in English-language searches — worth a Hebrew-specific App Store/Google Play search in round 2 to see if this niche is already served (competitive angle, but also indicates whether "holiday countdown" is a recognized use case Israeli users already search for).

## Could not find

- No Israel-specific consumer survey or study on how Israelis track Jewish/Israeli holidays digitally today (comparing Hebcal-style apps, physical/paper calendars, WhatsApp reminders, and native phone calendar habits side-by-side).
- No independent (non-vendor-produced) quantitative study of countdown-app-specific user demographics or usage behavior — all quantitative "digital calendar" stats traced back to a single vendor (ECAL), which does not satisfy the two-source rule.
- No app-store category-level report (App Annie/data.ai, Sensor Tower, Similarweb, etc.) specifically sizing the "countdown apps" category (installs, revenue, DAU) globally or in Israel with credible independent sourcing — only paid-analytics tool landing pages were surfaced, not actual published figures.
- No data on physical/paper Hebrew wall-calendar (לוח שנה עברי) sales trends in Israel, to compare digital vs. analog adoption for holiday tracking.
