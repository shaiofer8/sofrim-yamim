## Findings

- **Mainstream smartphone calendar apps (e.g. Google Calendar) cannot easily handle recurring annual events pinned to the Hebrew calendar date — a Hebrew tech-lifestyle article states plainly: "קושי לעשות זאת עם לוחות השנה שבטלפון החכם" (difficulty doing this with the calendars built into the smartphone) when trying to set a recurring Hebrew-date birthday or yahrzeit/memorial-day reminder.** [Source: TGSpot, "שנה עברית בפתח: כל הדרכים לנהל לוח שנה עברי בטלפון," accessed 2026-08-17, https://www.tgspot.co.il/hebrew-calander-in-your-phone/]. Confidence: medium-high (direct fetch and read of the article succeeded; this is a primary editorial source explicitly describing the gap, not a search synthesis). Class: pain-point.

- **The workaround culture around this gap is real but clunky: the article describes a dedicated tool/app ("ניהול תאריך עברי") whose only function is to compute which Gregorian date a given Hebrew date falls on each year, so the user can then manually re-add the event to their phone calendar — i.e., users are patching a hole in mainstream calendar apps with a second, single-purpose tool.** [Source: TGSpot, "שנה עברית בפתח: כל הדרכים לנהל לוח שנה עברי בטלפון," accessed 2026-08-17, https://www.tgspot.co.il/hebrew-calander-in-your-phone/]. Confidence: medium-high. Class: pain-point.

- **A separate outlet documents a developer (Ariel Schreiber) who built a dedicated app ("Facebook Jewish Birthday") specifically because Facebook's Gregorian-only birthday reminders cause real social friction: friends greet Hebrew-date birthday celebrants on the wrong day, leading to hurt/frustrated reactions ("תגובות זועפות") from people expecting a greeting on their actual (Hebrew) birthday.** [Source: Kipa, "אפליקציה חדשה תזכיר לכם את ימי ההולדת העבריים," accessed 2026-08-17, https://www.kipa.co.il/%D7%AA%D7%A8%D7%91%D7%95%D7%AA/%D7%90%D7%A4%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%A7%D7%A6%D7%99%D7%94-%D7%97%D7%93%D7%A9%D7%94-%D7%AA%D7%96%D7%9B%D7%99%D7%A8-%D7%9C%D7%9B%D7%9D-%D7%90%D7%AA-%D7%99%D7%9E%D7%99-%D7%94%D7%94%D7%95%D7%9C%D7%93%D7%AA-%D7%94/]. Confidence: medium-high (direct primary-article read). Class: pain-point.

- **Taken together, these two independent editorial sources (TGSpot and Kipa) corroborate a real, recurring underserved need — mainstream tools do not natively support Hebrew-date-anchored recurring reminders, and Israeli/Jewish users have historically resorted to niche single-purpose apps (Hebcal, "ניהול תאריך עברי," "Facebook Jewish Birthday") to patch the gap rather than finding it solved inside a general countdown/event app.** [Source: synthesis of TGSpot and Kipa articles above, both accessed 2026-08-17]. Confidence: medium (inference across 2 sources meets the two-source rule for a "pattern," though it is a pattern about calendar apps generally, not about countdown apps specifically — flagged as a lead for a differentiation angle, not a proven countdown-category gap). Class: pain-point.

- **Widget unreliability is a broad, cross-app pattern beyond the two apps already logged in Rounds 1–2: reported issues include a widget frozen on a stale count until the app is manually reopened, a widget still displaying an old/deleted event's date after the user recreated the counter, and a widget that "stopped working" after an OS/app update.** [Source: Google Search result synthesis across multiple Apple App Store countdown-widget listings (StarDay: Countdown Widget; Inauguration Countdown Widget; Countdown Widgets: Counter App), accessed 2026-08-17, https://apps.apple.com/us/app/starday-countdown-widget/id1530899550]. Confidence: medium (iOS-side evidence; corroborates the Android-side widget complaints from Round 1 as a cross-platform category-wide pattern, class of app rather than one buggy app). Class: pain-point.

## Leads

- The Hebrew-date recurring-reminder gap is the clearest, best-corroborated candidate for a genuine "underserved niche" angle for Sofrim Yamim — but note it is evidenced from the *Hebrew calendar app* category and general smartphone-calendar behavior, not from reviews of the specific countdown apps named in the brief (Countdown App - Days Counter, Big Day, Countdown Star, ChronoLite, Day Counter). No review of those specific apps was found complaining about missing Hebrew/Jewish holiday presets — this is an inferred/adjacent gap, not a directly observed complaint in the named apps' own review sections.
- Worth a follow-up (different dimension/round) checking Hebrew Play Store reviews of Israeli-made calendar/countdown apps specifically for the phrase "תאריך עברי" or "יום הולדת עברי" combined with "לא ניתן" / "אין אפשרות" (i.e., explicit "can't do X" complaints) to convert this from an inferred gap into a directly observed one.
- Widget reliability, now corroborated across 4+ distinct apps (2 Android-side from Round 1, 2+ iOS-side from Round 3) spanning both platforms, is a solid, broad category-wide weakness — a reliably working widget is a plausible differentiator, independent of the Hebrew-holiday angle.
